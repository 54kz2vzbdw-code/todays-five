# Today's Five — the 1.7 audit

Twelve lenses, one pass, no features. Eleven agents looked (accessibility had two) at build 76 through the environments in the brief; the orchestrator reproduced what they reported, fixed what was a defect, and ranked the rest here for the next round. The raw reports are in `audit/raw/`, the evidence in `audit/` (named by finding), the fixes in the appendix at the bottom with the finding that drove each one.

## The page

**What the app does well.** The core is one system, and six rounds have not bent it: a Today row is one control at rest and two on hover, an Everything row two and three, the footer has none, crossing off and adding a line are one tap each. Every colour and font is a token, so light and dark stay in step and the theme engine's floors held for every one of twenty random themes thrown at it. The document model is the strongest part of the code: merge is a real lattice join over four hundred random triples, rollover is clock-independent to the byte, import of an export is exactly normalize, and the busy-server path holds and retries exactly as designed. The secret stays in the fragment, never in a log, a title, a referrer or an export; a stranger on a View link cannot reach it from anywhere on their device; every one of twenty-odd HTML injection sites is fixed markup or escaped text. Idle cost is near zero by instrumentation, not inspection: twelve milliseconds of work in five real minutes, nothing written, and a 400-line list renders in 206 ms with no long task. The welcome is the best first minute this lens has seen in a small app — a real list that behaves exactly like the real thing — and the app's own accessibility decisions hold: a line is a real checkbox, the repeat glyph is spoken, sections are headings, deletion moves focus to a neighbour, reduced motion is honoured almost everywhere. The panel stack from 1.4 was the strongest thing the surfaces lens touched: one level per Back, the parent's scroll byte-identical, the hash untouched after every unwind.

**The five changes that would matter most** (ranked by impact against effort; each is a proposal below, with evidence):

1. **Skip makes an empty list when nothing was touched.** Today "Skip" keeps the three tutorial lines as the person's first real, synced list; the copy now says so, but the honest fix is an empty list. Low effort, every newcomer.
2. **The save sheet gets a way out.** It is the one panel with no ×; its only labelled exit is "I've saved it", a claim that switches the app's only nudge about the only key off for good. A × and a "Not yet" chip, both leaving the nudge on. Low effort, the first thirty seconds.
3. **A home day for a shared list.** Rollover uses each device's own midnight, so a phone in Tokyo files a Chicago laptop's line under yesterday at half past ten in the morning, and the line survives only as History text. Pin the day boundary to the document. Medium effort, the whole shared-list case.
4. **The gesture in half the time.** A check-off takes 1.04 s to come to rest with 170 ms of dead air in the middle; rows cross through each other during the sink; the knock is over before the ink is a third of the way across. Three numbers and one class. Low-to-medium effort, every tap.
5. **Settings and Lists, consolidated.** Settings' "Lists" group is three redirects, Lists offers Rename and Remove twice a row apart, and a line can be moved to another list but not to another section from its menu. Low effort, fewer taps every day.

## Proposals, ranked

Impact first, effort second; the first paragraph of each is the case, the evidence is the file named. "Waits" means it changes design and is Price's call.

1. **Skip creates an empty list when the welcome was not touched.** (impact high · effort low) The welcome's Skip hands the three tutorial lines over as the first real list; on a phone deleting them is three holds and three menu taps, and the first bookmarked or shared list reads as junk. The 1.3 decision "Keep is Skip with a reason" was about not losing the person's work, and Keep already covers that the moment there is work. When `#w-keep` is still hidden, Skip should create the list without the seed lines; when Keep is showing, Skip keeps its meaning. This round changed the label to "Skip — keep these lines" so it stops lying; the behaviour is the real fix. Evidence: `audit/skip-keeps-demo.png`. Mockup: `audit/mock-skip.png`.
2. **The save sheet gets a × and a "Not yet".** (high · low) `#p-save` is the only panel with no × and no Back. Escape and a swipe do close it, invisibly; the visible exit is "I've saved it", which flips `linkSaved` and removes the ⋯ "Save your link" row and its dot for good. A newcomer meets this sheet before "the link is the key" means anything. Add the standard × and a "Not yet" chip; both leave the nudge on, exactly as the 1.3 decision "Saved means copied or confirmed" wants. Evidence: `audit/save-sheet-no-exit.png`. Mockup: `audit/mock-save-sheet.png`.
3. **A home zone on the document, so a shared list rolls over once.** (high for shared lists · medium) `rollover(doc, today)` takes today from the device, so a list rolls at the earliest midnight among its devices; a device fourteen hours ahead tombstones a line crossed off two hours ago, and the tombstone is bare by design, so the line is not in Removed lines either. Store the list's zone (or offset) on the document at creation (an additive key, old clients pass it through, COMPATIBILITY.md §3) and compute both `today` and `localDate(doneAt)` in it; failing that, refuse to roll a line whose `doneAt` is under six hours old. Evidence: `audit/timezone-rollover.txt`.
4. **The check-off gesture, choreographed.** (medium-high · low-medium) Measured on the desktop: the strike ends at 432 ms, nothing happens until 598 ms, then a 520 ms sink; tap to rest is 1.04 s. Rows moving past each other draw on top of one another for ~150 ms (no background, no z-order). The knock is a 108 ms sound under a 380 ms stroke; the finale card is fully on screen 110 ms before the chord and the confetti. Proposed: `delay: 320` and a 300 ms FLIP so the sink starts as the last ink lands; the strike to .22 s with an .08 s stagger for wrapped lines; a `.moving` class with a background and a z-index (and a 20 ms stagger) so the reorder reads as one object passing another; the chord and the volley at ~260 ms with the card fading in under them. These change the app's motion character, which is why they wait. Evidence: `audit/tap-to-rest.png`, `audit/rows-crossing.png`, `audit/finale-timing.txt`.
5. **Settings' "Lists" group, and Lists' bottom row.** (medium · low) "Removed lists" opens the Lists panel that sits one row above in the same menu; "Templates" duplicates every section header's ⋯; "History" is a record among switches. Lists offers Rename and Remove at the bottom for the current list and again in each list's detail. Delete the Removed row, show Templates only when the list has no sections, move History to the list's detail, drop the two bottom buttons (keep New list and the paste field). Settings 19 → 16 controls; Lists 13 → 11. Lost: History one tap deeper, and one relearned place. Evidence: `audit/settings-lists-group.png`, `audit/lists-duplicates.png`.
6. **"Move to…" covers sections too.** (medium · low) Sections are the filing system, and filing means a long drag across seven sections on a phone; the rarer cross-list move has a row and a picker. Rename the row "Move to…" and give the existing pick sheet two groups: this list's sections, then the other lists on this device. The v4 decision already calls this row Move to…. Evidence: `audit/move-to-sections.png`. Mockups: `audit/mock-move-to.png` (the row), `audit/mock-move-to-picker.png` (the picker).
7. **The builder behind one row.** (medium · low) The theme picker and the builder are one 1683 px sheet on a phone; picking one of fourteen kits is the common job and it is the top third. The builder becomes "Make your own ›" under the swatches; 1.4's stack does the rest. Lost: one tap for frequent theme-makers. Evidence: `audit/builder-behind-a-row.png`.
8. **Settings regrouped by this device / this list.** (medium · low) "Behavior" is four unrelated device habits, "Lists" holds no settings, "Advanced" holds the only backup there is and a privacy switch about other people. Three groups: Appearance, This device (sound, pack, volume, celebrate, day review, keep awake, swipe, keys, fade, who's here), This list (export & import, templates if kept, add from anywhere). Evidence: `audit/settings-groups.png`. Mockups: `audit/mock-settings.png`, `audit/mock-settings-2.png`.
9. **The Share sheet's first block says it is for you.** (medium · low) The Private link marked `/mine` is the sheet's first block, its Copy the first Copy, and on the desktop its code the first thing drawn; a newcomer sharing with a partner takes it, and the partner's device files the list under My lists with no question. A qualifier beside the heading, the way "view only" sits beside "Show it somewhere": `Open on my other device · just for you`. Evidence: `audit/share-first-block.png`.
10. **The origin control as two answers.** (medium · low) After a wrong answer to Whose list is this?, the way back is a switch (now labelled "Mine") with its meaning in the sub-line. Mirror the question in the list's detail: "Whose list is this?" over the two answers. Evidence: `audit/origin-toggle.png`.
11. **The count as a control on phones.** (medium · low) Tapping the count opens one-thing mode, the biggest state change in the app, and on a phone nothing says it is a control (it now carries a description for the reader and a 44 px hit area). A first-time mark the first time the mode opens, or a visible affordance. Evidence: `audit/count-control.png`.
12. **"Shake to shuffle?" waits for the second visit, or names itself.** (low-medium · low) It stacks under the one-thing hint in the same second a newcomer discovered the mode by accident, asking to Allow something unnamed; × is remembered as declined. Hold it until the second visit, or "Shake the phone for a different line?" Evidence: `audit/shake-ask.png`.
13. **The losing side of a simultaneous edit gets a word.** (medium · medium) Last-writer-wins is right and the merge is correct, but the person whose words lost watches their line change with no explanation and no way back. When a pull replaces the text of a line this device edited in the last minute, toast once with an Undo that restores the pre-merge snapshot the engine already holds. Evidence: `audit/conflict-silent.png`.
14. **Lazy modules cache-first for the page's own build.** (medium on slow networks · low) A same-build `?v=` request falls through to network-first, so on a slow connection the first Settings, Share and Export pay their fetch on every visit; the content is immutable by construction (a new build gets a new number and a new cache). Serve `+v === BUILD` from this build's cache first, network as the fallback. This changes §6's policy for those files, so it waits. Evidence: `audit/lazy-cache-first.json`.
15. **The worker's cache stores a quarter of its bytes twice.** (low · low) `"./"` and `"./index.html"` are both precached, and each lazy module sits under its plain and its `?v=` name: about 197 of 856 KB duplicated. This round fixed the part that mattered (the navigation key carried the list's link); the rest is tidiness. Evidence: `audit/sw-duplicates.json`.
16. **Framebusting in the boot script.** (low · low) A meta CSP cannot carry `frame-ancestors` and GitHub Pages sends no headers, so any site can frame the app and overlay its own UI on New keys or Delete everywhere (the attacker never learns the link). Two lines in the hashed boot script, then re-hash. Evidence: `audit/raw/privacy/report.md`.
17. **Strip bidi overrides from typed and imported text.** (low · low) U+202E passes through `str()` and reverses a line's reading on a shared list, where a reader trusts what they see. Strip U+202A–E and U+2066–69 in the editor's commit and in import. Evidence: `audit/bidi.txt`.
18. **Refuse an import that would make the list unsyncable.** (low · low) A 1.7 MB file parses, the label reads like success, the merge lands, and every push is then refused at the 96 KB cap. Seal the merged document first and compare with the cap; over it, say so and offer a new list instead. Evidence: `audit/import-size.txt`.
19. **The ⋯ "Theme" row.** (low · low) It opens Settings (at Appearance, per 1.2's decision) under a label the destination itself does not use; the keys sheet and How it works both say "Appearance". Rename the row, or open the picker for the live slot. Evidence: `audit/theme-row.png`.
20. **A heading for New keys.** (low · low) The fifth Share block is a bare button and a grey line; every other block says what you are doing. `Replace both links`, with the existing note. Evidence: `audit/new-keys-heading.png`.
21. **Drop the id fragment on Lists rows.** (low · low) Six characters of the id are the second most prominent thing on each row and mean nothing to a person (they are now hidden from the reader). The chevron already says there is more. Evidence: `audit/id-fragment.png`.
22. **A counter near the caps.** (low · low) The line editor stops at 200 characters and the note at 300 with no sign; on a phone that reads as the app freezing. A count in the last twenty characters, or an underline flash. Evidence: `audit/caps-counter.png`.
23. **The popovers share one anatomy.** (low · low) The ⋯ menu has icons, states and a red row; the line menu sub-lines and keys; the section menu neither. Pick one. Evidence: `audit/popover-anatomy.png`.
24. **A panel's title and its section headings are the same type.** (low · low) h2 and h3 differ by 0.22 px of tracking; "SETTINGS" and "APPEARANCE" read as peers. Give h2 a step. Evidence: `audit/h2-h3.png`.
25. **One hover treatment.** (low · low) A menu row fills, the × beside it draws a border, a swatch lights only its edge. Evidence: `audit/hover-treatments.png`.
26. **The idle fade to 0.2, not 0.** (low · low) The controls that fade become invisible-but-clickable ghosts for a slow-reading mouse user; at 0.15–0.25 the quiet survives. Evidence: `audit/idle-fade.png`.
27. **The rail at 200 % text on a phone.** (low · low) The list chip reads "W…" and the tab "EVERYT"; let the rail wrap or drop the date sooner. Evidence: `audit/text-200.png`.
28. **The toast's Undo at the end of the tab order.** (low · low) Focus goes to a neighbouring line on delete, as promised, and Undo sits past every remaining row; Cmd/Ctrl+Z works. Move focus to the action when a toast raises one, or say "Cmd+Z undoes". Evidence: `audit/undo-tab-order.txt`.
29. **A done line while lifted, and Pink's accent text.** (low · low) `--done` on `--ink-3` reads under 4.5:1 in six kits while a done row is dragged (never under 3:1); Pink, an original kit whose tokens are kept exactly, has its accent text at 4.01:1 on a hovered star. A `--done-2` like `--dim-2`, and a call on Pink. Evidence: `audit/done-on-ink3.json`.
30. **A Markdown export that lists a Today line once.** (low · low) Every starred line prints under `## Today` and again under its section. Mark them in place instead. Evidence: `audit/markdown-twice.txt`.
31. **A two-column ⋯ menu in phone landscape.** (low · low) The launcher meant to be one glance becomes a scroll at 390 px of height. Evidence: `audit/landscape-menu.png`.
32. **The create limit's address hinge.** (server; note only) The bucket is the network address, so a café shares twelve new lists an hour, and a bare `x-forwarded-for` would let a stranger burn a chosen bucket if Cloudflare's header were ever absent. Nothing on the server changes in this round; recorded for the next migration. Evidence: `audit/raw/privacy/report.md`.

Bugs found and not fixed in this round, for the next: **Settings and the Today/Everything toggle retain nodes and listeners** (30 Settings open/close cycles kept 199 nodes and 55 listeners after a forced GC; 50 view switches 104 and 44; the theme picker, on the same panel machinery, kept 3 and 0) — the retaining reference needs a three-snapshot heap diff, which the budget did not allow; evidence `audit/leak.json`.

Considered and left as designed: a View link asks Whose list is this? (a view link on your own work computer is yours; the answer files it under My lists); the sync dot on a phone is a dot alone only while healthy (the label appears for every trouble state, as the v3 decision promised); "Tap or click to cross this off" and the other seed lines stay as 1.1 wrote them; the URL-shaped placeholders in the paste fields stay (they show what a link looks like).

## What no agent could verify

No screen reader ran here: every announcement finding comes from Chrome's accessibility tree and MutationObservers on the live regions, and the VoiceOver double-tap finding was argued from hit-testing. No real iPhone or iPad: the simulators ran Safari 18.1 and 26.5 through WebDriver (DOM clicks, not taps; on 26.5 not a gesture for audio), the Home Screen app could not be planted, and neither Larger Text nor the Compact/Bottom/Top Safari layouts were reachable. macOS Safari refused automation (Allow Remote Automation is off; no setting was changed). The live server was never called except by the real-backend suite (6 of 6), so the 413 and 507 paths, the realtime channel and presence over a real socket are known only from the engine's reaction to those statuses. True FOUT needs a slow network, not `127.0.0.1`. The day review card was never rendered by any lens. The Settings and view-switch leak was measured but not traced.
